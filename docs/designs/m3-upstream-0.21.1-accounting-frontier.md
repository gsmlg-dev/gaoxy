## Ownership contract to make the proof executable

The unit of admission is an **attempt lease**, acquired before a payload enters
any managed worker, mailbox, Fetch call or upload stream. At most32 leases exist
per scope, covering admitted, preparing, rejected and uncertain attempts.
The acquire operation carries bounded scalar identity only and cannot itself
queue unlimited callers. In the fixture, use a fixed bounded producer set and
an immediate gate; a flood must not spawn one waiter per rejected request.
Production must relate its pre-existing request admission to the same gate.

The lease progresses through `reserved -> preparing -> launched -> releasing
-> released`; failures can enter `cleanup_hold` from any allocated phase.
Represent individual edges explicitly in the model; the arrows are not a
claim that every successful request visits every phase. A lease reference and
generation identify every terminal transition. Duplicate or stale replies are
idempotent, cannot refund a newer lease, and contain no retained payload.

Source ownership is part of the state: prior source, canonical output,
upstream normalized copy, worker captures, stream messages and downstream
consumer each have a named owner. Enforce source backing before sending it;
copying a slice after receipt does not erase the earlier peak. One upload
producer may have one outstanding nonempty chunk≤65,536 bytes, with its source
read and next-read overlap separately charged. One response consumer releases
or transfers the previous chunk to a separately reserved bounded owner before
ACK. Count final trailers, terminal messages and error roots as well as data.

All attempts retain their lease until both public request completion and
Gaoxy-owned helper/source release are confirmed. Public completion alone does
not establish that old controls in a long-lived upstream pool have disappeared;
P5 must provide the separate history-independent frontier. Rejection before a
Promise still needs its preparation owner to release captures before refund.
Timeout, owner death and `cleanup_unconfirmed` retain capacity. Saturating
uncertain slots may stop new work; falsely refunding them is not a remedy.

Bound local control producers by operation identity. One scope has one opening
custodian, at most one retirement initiation and one abort escalation. A request
has one terminal cleanup coordinator and at most one abort initiation. Initial
completion wait and any finite cleanup-headroom wait are explicit finite
operations with charged helpers; retries from API callers join cached state.
Do not repeatedly call abort-and-await on every poll. Fixture snapshots are
bounded observations with fixed purpose/count, not a periodic source of calls.
Any later diagnostic polling needs its own outstanding-operation proof.
The absolute success deadline never restarts; late release cannot change a
timed-out result to success. These restrictions apply to controlled Gaoxy use,
not to the upstream library's unrestricted public API.

For aggregate ownership, charge preparing, published and retiring generations
before their resources exist. The numeric expression must include the bounded
manager/custodian/preparation infrastructure and failed slots. Require
`B_manager + sum(B_reserved_generation) <= configured_transport_budget` at
admission, with finite generation/participant counts and positive per-generation
reservation. Neither a per-scope R limit nor a byte gate whose minimum is zero
bounds an unlimited number of empty scopes. Preserve the existing64-participant
instance bound; it is not a substitute for the generation ledger.
